How this works

This site republishes public records about money, influence and government, and links every entry back to the official source it came from.

The rules

  1. Official records only. Entries come from government filings and databases such as the FEC, the House Clerk and USAspending.gov.
  2. A person checks every entry. Software collects records and flags patterns, and nothing is published until a reviewer opens the original source and confirms the entry matches it.
  3. Every entry is archived. Each source is saved to the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine so it can be checked even if the original page changes.
  4. We say what the records show. A filing showing a payment is reported as a payment. We do not claim motives the records don't show.
  5. Same standard for everyone. The same rules apply regardless of party, company or cause.
  6. Private people stay private. We track public officials, registered organizations and paid campaigns, and leave out private individuals' personal details.
  7. Corrections are public. Any change after publishing is listed on the corrections page with the date and reason.

Polls

Reader polls show what visitors want tracked next. They are unscientific, limited to one vote per device, and are not a measure of public opinion.

Open data

All published entries can be downloaded as JSON or CSV, and followed by RSS.
